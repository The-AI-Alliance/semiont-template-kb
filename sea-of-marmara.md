# Sea of Marmara

The Sea of Marmara (Turkish: Marmara Denizi) is a small inland sea that forms a critical link in the Turkish Straits system, connecting the Black Sea to the Mediterranean Sea. Located entirely within the borders of modern Turkey, this strategic waterway serves as both a geographical boundary between Europe and Asia and a vital maritime corridor that has shaped regional history for millennia.

## Geographical Characteristics

The Sea of Marmara covers approximately 11,350 square kilometers (4,382 square miles) and measures roughly 280 kilometers (174 miles) in length from east to west, with a maximum width of about 80 kilometers (50 miles). The sea reaches depths of up to 1,370 meters (4,446 feet) in its deepest basin, though much of the sea floor is considerably shallower.

The sea is connected to the Black Sea in the northeast through the Bosporus Strait, a narrow channel approximately 30 kilometers long that runs through Istanbul. To the southwest, the Dardanelles Strait (approximately 61 kilometers long) connects the Sea of Marmara to the Aegean Sea, and thus to the broader Mediterranean. This strategic position makes the Sea of Marmara an essential component of the only maritime route between the Black Sea and the world's oceans.

The sea contains several islands, the largest being the Marmara Islands (from which the sea derives its name, referring to the marble quarried there in antiquity), as well as Avşa Island and Imralı Island. The surrounding coastline features important Turkish cities including Istanbul, Bursa, and İzmit.

## Oceanographic Features

The Sea of Marmara exhibits a distinctive two-layer water structure resulting from the density differences between the less saline Black Sea water and the more saline Mediterranean water. Surface water flows from the Black Sea through the Bosporus into the Sea of Marmara and continues toward the Mediterranean, while a deeper, denser undercurrent flows in the opposite direction from the Mediterranean toward the Black Sea. This bidirectional flow creates unique ecological conditions and influences the marine environment of both connected seas.
